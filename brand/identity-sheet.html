The mark

The roundel stands alone once the name is already on the page — a social avatar, a watermark on a pedigree sheet, the corner of a photo. The crest is the ceremonial version: gate signs, jacket backs, the cover of a bull sale catalog. Both monochrome cuts exist for the places color can't go — a branding iron, embroidery, a single-color ear tag print.

Clear space & size

Clear space equals the radius of the roundel on all four sides. Nothing crosses that line — no photo edge, no sale date, no auctioneer's phone number.

Minimum size: 180 px or 1.5 in wide for the horizontal lockup; 24 px for the roundel alone. On a printed ear tag or a catalog footer, drop to the mark and set the ranch name in type beside it rather than shrinking the full lockup.

Usage

Do

  • Use the reverse files on anything darker than Ranch Grey.
  • Keep the head black. It is the breed; it doesn't take the brand color.
  • Scale from the SVG for anything printed larger than a letterhead.
  • Pair with photography of cattle on grass — the mark sits in a solid field, never directly on a busy image.

Don't

  • No recoloring the bull to blue, grey, or brand-of-the-season.
  • No stretching — scale both axes together, always.
  • No re-typesetting the wordmark at different tracking or in a different face.
  • No effects — bevels, drop shadows, gradients and outer glows all read as homemade on a seedstock mark.
  • No mid-blue grounds. The royal ring disappears into them.
