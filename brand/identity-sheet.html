Identity system — September 2026

Drawn from the hand-painted sign on the ranch trailer: a Western slab wordmark, white letters carrying a keyline, the two irons standing either side, and a script line for the country. The sign's teal becomes royal blue. The bull is drawn polled — no horns — which is how the breed reads to a cattleman at a glance.

Two colors, either way round

The wordmark is never flat — the keyline is the identity. On black it is white letters with a royal keyline, exactly as the sign is painted. On paper the same pair swaps, so the fill still dominates and Rye's spurred serifs stay crisp down to catalog sizes.

The irons

X7 and bar-7, set in the same Western slab as the wordmark so they sit with it rather than beside it. They flank the sign plate and the crest, and stand alone on tags, gates and stationery. Traced from the sign — send a correction if either differs from the registered brand and every file picks it up.

The mark

The roundel stands alone once the name is already on the page — a social avatar, a watermark on a pedigree sheet, the corner of a photo. The crest is the ceremonial version: gate signs, jacket backs, sale-day print. Both monochrome cuts exist for the places color can't go — a branding iron, embroidery, a single-color ear tag print.

Clear space & size

Clear space equals the radius of the roundel on all four sides. On the sign plate, the printed border is the clear space. Nothing crosses either line — no photo edge, no sale date, no auctioneer's phone number.

Minimum size: 200 px or 1.75 in wide for the horizontal lockup, 3 in for the sign plate, 24 px for the roundel alone. On a printed ear tag or a catalog footer, drop to the mark and set the ranch name in type beside it rather than shrinking the full lockup.

Usage

Do

  • Use the reverse files on anything darker than Ranch Grey.
  • Keep the head black (white when reversed). It is the breed, not an accent.
  • Keep the keyline. Flat one-color type is not this wordmark.
  • Scale from the SVG for anything printed larger than a letterhead.
  • Pair with photography of cattle on grass — the mark sits in a solid field, never directly on a busy image.

Don't

  • No recoloring the bull to blue, grey, or brand-of-the-season.
  • No stretching — scale both axes together, always.
  • No re-typesetting the wordmark at different tracking or in a different face.
  • No dropping the irons from the sign plate — they are the ranch's registered marks.
  • No effects — bevels, drop shadows, gradients and outer glows all read as homemade on a seedstock mark.
  • No mid-blue grounds. The royal ring disappears into them.

Typography

Three faces, each doing one job. Every letter in the logo files is an outline path — there is no live text and no font reference in the artwork, so it renders the same on any machine, any browser, any print shop.
